4 Theoretical model

4.1 Parasite load and infectiousness

4.1.1 1 bite, all human treated

Call \(t\) the time post drug administration in treated humans and \(\tau\) the time post mosquito infection. Call \(\alpha(t, \tau)\) the transmission blocking activity (TBA) defined as the percentage reduction of the number of mosquitoes infected consecutively to 1 blood meal at times \(t\) and \(\tau\). Call \(\beta(t, \tau)\) the transmission reducing activity (TRA) defined as the percentage reduction of the parasite load in infected mosquitoes consecutively to 1 blood meal at times \(t\) and \(\tau\). The mean reduction factor of the parasite load due to TBA and TRA and across the population of infected mosquitoes at times \(t\) and \(\tau\) then reads

\[ (1 - \alpha(t, \tau))(1 - \beta(t, \tau)). \]

Call \(h(t)\) is the density of probability of the distribution of times post drug administration in the population of treated humans. The mean reduction factor \(l(\tau, m)\) of parasite load across the population of infected mosquitoes due to 1 bite on treated people at time \(\tau\) then reads

\[ l(\tau, m) = \int_0^{m}(1 - \alpha(t, \tau))(1 - \beta(t, \tau))h(t)dt. \]

when we consider people who have been infected over the past \(m\) hours. Whenever we would need \(m = +\infty\) in theory, a value of \(m\) large enough so that \(\alpha(t,\tau) = \beta(t,\tau)\simeq 0\) would be enough in practice.

4.1.2 Multiple bites, \(p\%\) human treated

Call \(a\) the mosquito bite rate, \(p(m)\) the proportion of the human population that has received treatment over the past \(m\) hours and \(\lambda(\tau,m)\) the mean cumulative reduction factor of the parasite load due to all the bites that have occurred until time \(\tau\). The fact that each bite on a treated human reduces the parasite load of \((1 - l(\tau,m)) \%\) is conveyed by the following differential equation

\[ \frac{d\lambda}{d\tau} = -(1 - l(\tau, m)) a p(m) \lambda(\tau, m). \]

If we now assume that mosquitoes get infected by bites on untreated humans only then

\[ \lambda(0, m) = 1, \]

which leads to the following expression of the mean cumulative reduction factor of the parasite load at time \(\tau\)

\[ \lambda(\tau, m) = \exp\left[-ap(m)\int_0^\tau(1 - l(u,m))du\right] \]

4.1.3 Mean infectiousness reduction

Call \(\zeta(\lambda)\) the function that converts a reduction factor of parasite load \(\lambda\) into a reduction factor of mosquito infectiousness and \(\mu\) the mosquito death rate assumed to be independent of the mosquito’s age. The mean reduction factor of mosquito infectiousness in the total mosquito population due to TBA and TRA can then be expressed as

\[ \theta = \int_0^{+\infty}\mu e^{-\mu\tau}\zeta(\lambda(\tau,m))d\tau. \]

4.2 Parasite maturation and EIP

Call \(z(\tau)\) the proportion of infectious mosquitoes at time \(\tau\) post infection and assume it increases logistically from some arbitrary value close to 0 right after infection to 1 when all the mosquitoes are infectious. Under the same assumption as above that mosquitoes get infected by bites on untreated humans only then

\[ z(\tau) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{n\nu(0) - \tau\nu(\tau)}} \]

where the growth rate \(\nu\) depends on \(\tau\) accounting for the fact it gets reduced by any blood meal taken on treated patients and \(n\) is the EIP in absence of treatment. By definition, the EIP \(T\) is the mean time post infection at which the parasite reaches maturity for transmission, thus implicitly defined as

\[ z(T) = \frac{1}{2}. \] We can verify that, in absence of treatment (i.e. \(\nu(\tau) = \nu(0)\), \(\forall\tau\)), \(z(n) = 1/2\).

4.2.1 1 bite, all human treated

Call \(\gamma(t, \tau)\) the reduction factor of the maturation rate consecutively to 1 blood meal at times \(t\) and \(\tau\). The mean reduction factor \(q(\tau,m)\) of the maturation rate across the population of infected mosquitoes due to 1 bite on treated people at time \(\tau\) then reads

\[ q(\tau,m) = \int_0^{m} \gamma(t, \tau) h(t) dt \]

4.2.2 Multiple bites, \(p\%\) human treated

In a similar way as done above for the parasite load, we can express the fact that each bite on treated human reduces the maturation rate by \((1 - q(\tau,m))\)% by the following differential equation

\[ \frac{d\nu}{d\tau} = -(1 - q(\tau,m))ap(m)\nu(\tau,m) \]

which leads to the following expression of the mean cumulative reduction factor of the maturation rate:

\[ \kappa(\tau,m) = \exp\left[-ap(m) \int_0^\tau (1-q(s,m))\,ds\right] \]

Now that we have this, we can implicitly express the average EIP \(T\) in presence of the treatment in the human population as the value \(T\) satisfying

\[ T\kappa(T,m) = n \]

4.3 \(R_0\) reduction effect

Putting previous results together, if \(R_0\) is the value of the basic reproduction number in absence of treatment, then, in the presence of treatment in the population, its expression becomes

\[ \theta e^{-(T - n)\mu} R_0 \]

5 Experimental data

Let’s use the following 4-parameter functional relationship for \(\alpha(t, \tau)\), \(\beta(t, \tau)\) and \(\gamma(t, \tau)\):

\[ f(t,\tau) = A \cdot (1 - e^{-kt})\cdot e^{-(dt + r\tau)} \]

whereby, as function of \(t\), the drug effects are characterized by an initial saturating increase (captured by \(k\)) followed by an exponential decrease (captured by \(d\)) reflecting the PK/PD of the drug and, as function of \(\tau\), the effect is characterized by an exponential decrease (captured by \(r\)) simply reflecting the growth of the parasite (and thus the decrease of the relative concentration of the drug in each blood meal). Parameter \(A\) account for the maximum value reached.

A version of the model at 4 dpi:

f4dpi <- function(t, A, k, d) A * (1 - exp(-k * t)) * exp(-d * t)

A function that looks for initial values at 4 dpi:

initial_values <- function(x, y) {
  df <- tibble(x, y) |> 
    arrange(x)

  df2 <- df |> 
    group_by(x) |> 
    summarise(y = mean(y))
  
  A <- df2 |> 
    pull(y) |> 
    max()
  
  xs <- df2 |> 
    filter(y == A) |> 
    pull(x)

  k <- df |> 
    filter(x < first(xs)) |> 
    lm(y ~ -1 + x, data = _) |> 
    coef() |> 
    last()
  
  d <- df |>
    filter(x > last(xs)) |> 
    filter(y > 0) |> 
    mutate(across(y, log)) |> 
    lm(y ~ x, data = _) |> 
    coef() |> 
    last()
  
  c(A = A, k = k / A, d = -d)
}

A function that calibrates the model at 4 dpi with least sum of squares:

calibrate4dpi <- function(x, y) {
  optim(initial_values(x, y), function(p) sum((y - f4dpi(x, p[1], p[2], p[3]))^2))$par
}

boot_calibration_4dpi <- function(data, N = 1e4, eps = 1.1) {
  model <- data |> 
    stratified_bootstrap(eps * N) |> 
    map(~ with(.x, safely(calibrate4dpi)(t, value)))

  model[map_lgl(model, ~ is.null(.x$error))] |>
    map("result") |> 
    sample(N)
}

Putting everything together to generate the reduction function:

reduction1 <- function(alpha, beta, delta, n, mu, a, p, m, infinity) {
  l <- function(tau, m) {
    map_dbl(tau,
            ~ integrate2(function(x) (1 - alpha(x, .x)) * (1 - beta(x, .x)), 0, m) / m)
  }
  
  qf <- function(tau, m) {
    map_dbl(tau,
            ~ integrate2(function(x) size2rate_reduction(1 - delta(x, .x)), 0, m) / m)
  }
  
  lambda <- function(tau, a, p, m) {
    map_dbl(tau, ~ exp(-a * p * integrate2(function(x) 1 - l(x, m), 0, .x)))
  }
  
  kappaf <- function(tau, a, p, m) {
    map_dbl(tau, ~ exp(-a * p * integrate2(function(x) 1 - qf(x, m), 0, .x)))
  }
  
  theta <- function(mu, a, p, m, infinity) {
    integrate2(function(x) dexp(x, mu) * zeta(lambda(x, a, p, m)), 0, infinity)
  }
  
  EIPf <- function(n, a, p, m) {
    optimise(function(x) abs(x * kappaf(x, a, p, m) - n), c(0, 24 * 100))$min
  }
  
  theta(mu, a, p, m, infinity) * exp((n - EIPf(n, a, p, m)) * mu)  
}

6.2 Treatment of infected

The equilibrium proportion \(\bar{S}\) of susceptible (to clinical infection) in the population:

\[ \bar{S} = \frac{1 + ac/\mu}{R_0 + ac/\mu} \]

where \(c\) is the efficiency of transmission from human to mosquito. R code:

susceptible <- function(R0, c, a, mu) {
  tmp <- a * c / mu
  (1 + tmp) / (R0 + tmp)
}

The proportion of susceptibles that gets a clinical infection over the past period of time \(m\):

\[ P_m = 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{(R_0-1)}{1+ac/\mu}rm\right) \]

where \(r\) is the human recovery rate. R code:

proba_infection <- function(R0, r, m, a, c, mu) {
  1 - exp(-r * m * (R0 - 1) / (1 + a * c / mu))
}

Combining the two to compute the proportion of clinical cases over the past period of time \(m\):

\[ P_m\bar{S} = \frac{1+ac/\mu}{R_0+ac/\mu} \left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{(R_0-1)}{1+ac/\mu}rm\right)\right] \]

R code:

incidence <- function(R0, c, a, mu, r, m)  {
  tmp1 <- a * c / mu
  tmp2 <- tmp1 + 1
  tmp2 * (1 - exp(-r * m * (R0 - 1) / tmp2)) / (R0 + tmp1)
}

dynamics <- function(R0i = 40, n = 10, mu = 1 / 14, a = 1 / 3, prop = 1, m = 50,
                     cv = .3, r = 1 / 200, infinity = 250, nb = 10) {
  R0 <- p <- rep(0, nb + 1)
  R0 <- R0_tmp <- R0i
  for (i in 2:(nb + 1)) {
    p[i - 1] <- p_tmp <- incidence(R0_tmp, cv, a, mu, r, m)
    R0[i] <- R0_tmp <- reduction(n, mu, a, prop * p_tmp, m, infinity) * R0i
  }
  list(R0 = R0, p = head(p, -1))
}
